## 2. Suggestions (new; none repeat 10-18 → 11-26)

1. **Turn the 76 "## Mistakes" tables into one ledger, and track the share that no gate caught.** Every `corpus/lessons/round-NN.md` from r24 to r100 has a `## Mistakes` table with a `captured-by` column. That column is the lane's best measure of whether its gates *anticipate* failures. Nobody has ever totaled it. I parsed it today: **421 rows across 64 rounds, under 15 different header spellings** (`class | evidence pointer | captured-by | fix` ×39, `id / class | … | fix / disposition` ×6, etc.). 324 rows name a gate, 5 say "partly", and **92 (22%) say `none`**. The decade trend is not monotone. r21–30 had 18/36 none, r71–80 had 0/65, and r81–100 is back to 18/83. At least 40 of the "gate" rows actually credit a person (L1 reading the console, the operator, the verifier), as in r99 M2 ("Caught by L1 reading the console"). So the real no-mechanism rate is higher than 22%. The r71–80 zero looks like a labelling convention, not a fact.
   Proposal: add `corpus/mistakes.jsonl` (round, id, class, evidence, `captured_by` ∈ {gate:Qnn, human:L1|operator|verifier, none}, fix, `shown_able_to_fail` bool), backfilled by a `plans/mistakes_extract.py` that handles the 15 header variants. Add a lint that fails a new lessons file whose Mistakes table doesn't match one canonical header. Report "mechanism-capture rate" (gate / all) per round in SCORECARD's trend verdicts. This is different from 11-24 §2.1, which covered families for *seat findings* in routed-findings.jsonl. This covers the lane's *own process mistakes*, and whether its Q-gates or humans are doing the catching.

2. **Grade the verifier: record author-vs-verifier verdict pairs and whether a confirmed verdict was later overturned.** There are 73 `round-NN-verifier-spotcheck.md` files, each with `### Per-axis verdicts` and bold `**Verdict: …**` lines, alongside the author's verdicts in `round-NN.md`. The lane already reports "agree"-family words about 555 times and "disagree"-family words about 190 times across those files. But that is prose. No table records which claims the verifier contested, and none records which verifier-*confirmed* claims needed a dated correction later. The r93→r96 freeze edit and r99's two post-T corrections are examples, per 2026-11-13.md §1. A non-author verifier is the lane's main calibration instrument, and its error rate is unmeasured.
   Proposal: add `plans/verdict_pairs.py` that emits `.ufo/verdict-pairs.tsv` (round, claim, author_verdict, verifier_verdict, agree, later_corrected_in, sha). Pair the bold verdict lines per claim, and detect `later_corrected_in` from SCORECARD/LOG dated-correction entries that cite the claim id. Then report two numbers: the verifier's disagreement rate and its *miss rate* (agreed, later corrected). A miss rate near zero with low disagreement means the verifier is good. A high miss rate means it rubber-stamps, and the r101+ verifier brief should change. This is different from 11-13 §2.3 (re-measure grader verdicts myself) and 10-23 (gate yield). This one measures the human/agent verifier, not the gates or the grader.
