## 2. Suggestions (new; none repeat 10-18 → 11-19)

Both ideas are about what a caller pays to load this skill. They are measured on today's `SKILL.md`: 371,469 B, 1,521 lines, 23 headings.

1. **Move the gate/probe registry out of SKILL.md and give the skill body a byte budget.** The section `## Gate/probe registry (PROTOCOL §Gate standard)` (lines 592–889) is **235,574 B, or 63% of SKILL.md**. It has 184 table rows (85 `gate`, 99 `probe`) averaging about 1.2 KB each. The longest rows are 2.6–3.4 KB (lines 631–692). Operators need this ledger, but callers do not. The parts a caller routed here actually uses are the three decision tables (lines 23–91, about 14.5 KB), the anti-patterns (92–210, about 8 KB) and the production checklist (890–932, about 2.8 KB). Together that is about 25 KB, or roughly 7% of the bytes. Loading the skill whole costs about 90k tokens, so a router pays roughly 13 tokens of registry and round history for every token of advice. The per-round TB2 grader sections (lines 978–1521, rounds 28–44, about 80 KB) have the same shape. They also stop at r44, even though the recipe table already cites up to r100.
   Proposal: move the registry verbatim to `REGISTRY.md`, or generate it there from grader metadata, and leave a one-paragraph pointer in SKILL.md. Move the r28–r44 sections into `corpus/knowledge/tb2-recipes/<class>.md`, linked from the recipe-table rows. Then add a grader `check_skill_budget`: it fails when SKILL.md exceeds a fixed cap, for example 60 KB, and when any registry-table row appears in it. The selftest would append one registry row and expect FAIL. This is not the 11-15 §2.2 idea, which trimmed absent *paths* from the description, and it is not the 11-10 PROVINCE split, which covered a different file. This one bounds the context cost of the front door itself.
2. **Give registry rows a lifecycle status, so the standing gates can be told apart from frozen round instruments.** 141 of the 184 registry names are round-scoped (`roundNN-*` / `rNN`). Only 43 are standing tools (`hermetic-grader`, `coverage-census`, `interval-reverdict`, `ledger-tip`, `routed-findings`, …). Just 13 rows mention retired, superseded or archived anywhere in their text. Nothing tells a caller, or a future round, which of the 85 gates still guard something today and which are historical receipts. The registry also has one `gate` row whose self-test cell reads "none": `round100-prefreeze-check`, the newest gate.
   Proposal: add a `status` column (`standing` | `round-frozen` | `retired→<successor>`). Then add a grader `check_registry_status` that (a) requires the column on every row, (b) requires every round-named row older than the current round to be `round-frozen` or `retired`, and (c) requires every `standing` gate to declare a non-"none" self-test. That last rule would flag `round100-prefreeze-check` immediately, unless it is marked round-frozen. Combined with idea 1, `REGISTRY.md` could open with the 43 standing rows and archive the rest below them.
