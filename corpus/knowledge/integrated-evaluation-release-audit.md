# An integrated release memo must update claims separately

This is an ordered application of held round64/66–70 knowledge, not a new
statistical principle, benchmark or runtime capability. The useful artifact is a
claim-specific decision ledger: one document can have a valid finite-panel
positive, a failed purported recovery and a separately justified sequential
result. A universal PASS/HOLD stamp loses that distinction.

## Fill this memo in order

1. **Write the contract before computing.** Name exact policy/selector/grader,
   target population or finite panel, assigned unit, outcome, coverage operating
   point, utility and all-attempt cost. Identify the conjuncts required for the
   specific decision, and which attractive-looking statistics are not conjuncts.
2. **Bind original evidence to that contract.** Join assigned IDs, original
   outcomes, accepted IDs, policy/threshold/split, costs and custody. A hash checks
   equality to a trusted earlier commitment; it does not establish who committed
   it, when, or whether the semantics match. Shared generator and display name do
   not make a revised selector or tuned split the original experiment.
3. **Reconstruct three denominators.** Show C/W/R with N assigned, coverage
   (C+W)/N, retained risk W/(C+W), and correctly served C/N. Empty acceptance has
   undefined retained risk. Keep oracle candidate availability separate from the
   returned policy. At matched coverage compare the prechosen operating points,
   not whichever rows later look best. Different accepted IDs need not be an error.
4. **Bound what remains unknown.** Under fixed assigned N, observed gross U,
   unknown count m, outcome utility in[a,b] and complete total charge K, net
   utility per assigned item lies in[(U+ma-K)/N,(U+mb-K)/N]. This is a finite
   identification set, not a confidence interval. Additional restrictions may
   narrow it; missing cost requires its own bounds. Unknown does not mean failed.
5. **Name the smallest evidence repair and transition.** Recover originals with
   binding and policy provenance, preserve failures/costs, then recompute only the
   affected decisions. Reject counterfeit recovery without discarding independent
   genuine recovery. If every finite conjunct is met, accept that finite positive;
   absence of a population guarantee is not reason to erase a true finite result.
6. **Audit inference on a separate branch.** Identify the sequential experiment,
   full information set, conditional null, stake range/timing and capital/restart
   accounting. A different stream's valid crossing does not certify this panel's
   utility or A-versus-B superiority. Retain current wealth and historical crossing
   separately; do not reuse the raw peak as e-wealth.
7. **Write the scope of every result.** Output decision, evidence that changed
   it, remaining uncertainty and authority boundary. Operational runtime health
   needs actual logs/context; synthetic provenance stipulations are not runtime
   evidence. Deployment requires its own target-relevant evidence and authority.

## Source gate and boundary

S240's pinned code defines per-problem candidate success; S243 defines coverage
and conditional retained risk; S246 anchors conditional wealth and evidence
combination. Located short excerpts and read receipts are in source-openings.md.
S247 anchors original finite arithmetic and the scenario's assumed custody, not
external empirical validity. No source paper establishes these synthetic counts.

This extends the held component cards by making evidence-to-decision transitions
explicit. It does not supersede their definitions. It fails if a memo imports a
validity premise from an unrelated stream, substitutes matched utility for full
utility, treats a hash as custody, ignores a valid narrow positive, or upgrades
finite eligibility to deployment. No baseline proves causal training gain; no
new principle, numeric score, TB2 task, Q23/Q32/Q38 closure or runtime parity.
