## 6. What this source can and cannot support (one sentence)

This working paper can ground a per-benchmark contamination-transparency card's *normative and design* claim — that
item-level disclosure and procedural disclosure pull benchmark validity in opposite directions, and that disclosure
should attach to procedures, governance, evaluator credentials and contamination/validity testing rather than to live
item pools, plus the §8.2 field list such a card would ask a benchmark owner to declare — but it cannot support any
measured per-benchmark contamination statistic, any card format or scored instrument, or any observed disclosure
practice: it is a political-economy working paper with a formal model and a micro1 wage-premium dataset, contains no
tables and no contamination-rate measurements, and is not an evaluation-transparency standard or audit.
