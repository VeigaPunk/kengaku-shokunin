## Collision rulings

**(a) Kotoba vs kengaku — prompt-format effects.** Kengaku's round-7 wire capture/ablation is evaluation evidence: it isolates how system content, tool affordances, and decoding conditions affect output strategy or harness validity (`corpus/knowledge/bench-arena-round7-scaffold-ablation.md:15-39,52-123`). Round-8 is likewise an eval design comparing incremental state transcripts, checkpoint/ack turns, one-shot state output, corruption/hint conditions, and constraint-repair conditions (`corpus/evidence/round8-horizon/horizon.py:125-223,225-300,302-346`; run summary: `/tmp/bench-r8/run.log:80-91`). Its final scores remain kengaku evidence; the summary alone is not a prompt-craft prescription. Kotoba owns the reusable craft/render/trigger implications, grounded in its router-render analysis (`kotoba-shokunin/corpus/knowledge/render-mechanism-round7.md:34-94`). **Decision:** kengaku registers and scores prompt format as a controlled treatment; report actionable prompt-authoring lessons to kotoba, without moving the benchmark, scores, or calibration claims into kotoba.

**(b) Kumitate/kasegi/kagi vs kengaku — route telemetry.** Split by data producer and inference. Kengaku's local-model eval telemetry (response correctness, finish, usage, latency) and its model/reroute performance interpretation stay in kengaku (`kengaku-shokunin/SKILL.md:108-154`). Kasegi owns the bounty ledger's mission-state reading; kumitate owns OMP route resolution, fallback, and operational failure classification; kagi owns the Janitor's endpoint/config and credential boundary. The ledger split is already explicit, not a new round-9 claim (`.ufo/reports/round-3/aggregator.md:87-90`). Bailian CLI quota/auth records are provider/client-operation telemetry, not benchmark pass/fail: keep only model-service implications in kengaku; route OMP failure taxonomy to kumitate, and route credential-handling findings to kagi only when they concern its Janitor/vault boundary. Do not silently transfer all “telemetry” to one lane.

**(c) Kasegi vs kengaku — model catalog / endpoint probing.** External model-catalog schemas and model capability/identity probes are kengaku's LLM-research surface (`kengaku-shokunin/SKILL.md:251-257`; `corpus/README.md:39-47`). A bounty fleet registry mapping lane to model is an orchestration/routing concern, not model catalog research; the prior ruling assigns that to kumitate rather than kasegi (`.ufo/reports/round-3/aggregator.md:85-90`). For endpoint probes, kengaku owns the endpoint's model identity and scored behavior; kagi owns Janitor's consumer-side `baseURL`; serving-plane ownership remains unassigned in the prior fleet ruling (`.ufo/reports/round-4/aggregator.md:140-156`). **Decision:** do not absorb fleet routing or Janitor config fixes into kengaku, and do not re-label an operational route map as a model catalog.

**(d) Protocol/aggregator vs kengaku — SCORECARD/AXES interpretation.** `/home/vgpnk/projects/shokunin/AXES.md:7-32` defines the axes, one-move round discipline, and required `SCORECARD.md` fields (`axes`, `planned`, `retired`); `MISSION.md:24-32` assigns the axis-move and allows evidence-based axis changes. Protocol owns fleet-meta/gate integrity, and the aggregator reviews corpus quality (`MISSION.md:11-12`; `PROTOCOL.md:226-245`). **Decision:** protocol/AXES remains the contract source; kengaku owns its evidence-backed scores and planned move; aggregator reviews/cross-routes but cannot redefine the scorecard schema or reassign an axis by local interpretation.
