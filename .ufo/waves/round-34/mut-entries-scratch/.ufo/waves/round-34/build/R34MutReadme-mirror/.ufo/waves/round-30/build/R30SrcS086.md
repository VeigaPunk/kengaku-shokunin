## Contiguity caveat (bears on C3)

- **The ≤25-token ceiling is inert here.** 3, 4 and 3 tokens for the three located quotes; a whole Japanese sentence is 1–4 whitespace tokens. C3's `<=25 words` failure case will be exercised by Latin-script sources, not this one.
- **Byte identity is the live constraint.** Any Japanese sentence longer than one printed line is split by `pdftotext`'s line wrapping, so `quote in artifact_bytes` is **False** for a genuine quote unless the gate unwraps lines. Both of my primary quotes (a-primary, a-reason) are of that kind: they are exact substrings of `Q2-11.unwrapped.txt` (sha256 above) and **not** of `Q2-11.raw.txt`. A C3 gate fed `Q2-11.raw.txt` verbatim would refuse two of the three S086 quotes — a false refusal, not a false admission. Two admissible gate shapes: (i) normalise the artifact by removing line breaks before matching, or (ii) require the quote to sit inside one wrapped line (the fallback runs given above satisfy (ii)).
- **The artifact for a PDF row must be the extract, not the PDF.** No Japanese byte run exists in the 371155 PDF bytes; the gate therefore needs `--artifact Q2-11.raw.txt` (or the unwrapped file) plus the recorded PDF sha256 for provenance. Stating the pairing so the receipt can carry both hashes.
- Poppler version used: `pdftotext version 26.08.0` (host package). Line numbers and offsets in this report are tied to that version's output; a different poppler could re-wrap lines and shift them, while leaving the quote bytes themselves intact.

## What this source can and cannot support

**One sentence:** S086 can support card fields that record *which contamination-removal regime a reporter used and what its stated false-positive cost is* — because it measures overlap for 17 named code/math benchmarks, reports the magnitude (0.00056% of a 3.7-billion-file pretraining corpus), and states in its own words that uniform string-match removal is doubtful since short generic code (`return x+y`) occurs in harmless training data — but it cannot support any per-benchmark contamination *verdict* or any cross-domain generality, because it is a single-venue Japanese proceedings paper whose measurements cover GitHub-derived code pretraining for one 1.4B-parameter model and those 17 benchmarks (11 of which show any string overlap), its repository-provenance step applies only to datasets that name their repository (RepoBench, RepoQA), and it reports no per-benchmark card or disclosure protocol of its own.
