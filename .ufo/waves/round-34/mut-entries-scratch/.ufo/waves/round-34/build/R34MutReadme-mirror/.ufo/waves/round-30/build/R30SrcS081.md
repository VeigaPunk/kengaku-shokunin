Two consequences, both checked:
1. A quote taken from the paper body is **not** automatically a byte substring of the abs page, and
   vice versa: `We operationalize it …` occurs only in `abs.html`; `We operationalize the taxonomy …`
   occurs only in `full.html`/`paper.pdf`.
2. The abs-page abstract omits the `under 25% in every stratum` prediction entirely and reports the
   prevalence-robust outcome in a different form ("Two variables fall below" vs "three clear … the
   two that fall below it are"). A row whose `anchor` says `# Abstract` therefore points at two
   different texts depending on which artifact the reader opens.

A second, mechanical hazard for any byte-substring validator: `full.html` contains **173** U+00A0
non-breaking spaces (e.g. `CC\xc2\xa0BY\xc2\xa04.0`) and hard newlines inside paragraphs, and the PDF
text layer hyphenates across lines. `abs.html` has **0** non-breaking spaces and single-line
paragraphs, so it is the cleanest artifact for strict byte checks of the abstract quotes.

## 8. What S081 can and cannot support (one sentence)

This source can support, for a per-benchmark contamination-transparency card, the five-type taxonomy
it prints (direct / derivative / temporal / distributional / acquired, organized by the mitigation
each defeats), the design of a four-field score-side *and* release-side disclosure protocol released
with a JSON Schema, validator and worked examples, and the single load-bearing rule that acquired
evaluation-time contamination is "a property of one run" recorded with the reported score rather than
with the benchmark release — and it can support disclosure *gaps* as measured, not assumed
(elicitation budgets reported in 13% of documents, no document addressing all five types, per-variable
linear-weighted κ 0.00–0.35, median 0.21, pooling to 0.46) — but it cannot support any claim that such
a card changes evaluator behaviour, disclosure rates over time, or leaderboard decisions (no
intervention or outcome experiment is reported), cannot supply reliability better than its own
pre-registered coding study, and cannot be cited as peer-reviewed standing, being a single v1 preprint
of 29 Aug 2026.
