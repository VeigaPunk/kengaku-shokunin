# Grader perturbation audit — response-format invariance is conditional on the extraction policy

## Claim and scope

A grader's reproducible replay is not a test of whether its verdict depends on incidental response format.
The lane's `grader.py` replays stored `raw` through a last-fenced-block extractor, then exact state/list
comparison or the Latin-square constraints. A paired response perturbation can distinguish extraction
policy from model correctness, provided its polarity is established against the original task contract.
This entry extends `knowledge/llm-judge-calibration.md` and the coverage-map row **Grader robustness /
response-format invariance**; the attribution interval entry covers a different instrument/population.

This is **not a new method**: CheckList (S043, §2.2) defines invariance for model predictions after
label-preserving input changes; evalmut 0.1.0 (S045) already ships equivalent-response and defect-response
mutations against answer graders. xFinder (S044, Abstract) explains why RegEx-oriented evaluation can favor
responses easily extracted by RegEx; it is adjacent extraction research, not evidence that this audit's
grader is generally reliable. Novelty score is held at 7; the contribution is a byte-pinned lane-specific audit.

## Exact perturbation policy

- **F1 / F2:** remove / uppercase the language label of **only the last matched answer fence**, leaving
  earlier fences intact. Unmatched closing fences are not applicable. Removing every fence is a different
  sensitivity, not this registered transform.
- **F3:** replace the extracted answer's JSON representation with compact versus indented serialization.
- **F4:** reverse keys of the parsed dictionary (at least two keys); JSON duplicate keys have already been
  resolved by the decoder, so this is not reversal of duplicate key text.
- **F5 / F6:** add a bracket-free one-line prose prefix / suffix. The no-brackets restriction matters.
- **F7:** replace LF with CRLF where line breaks exist. S046 documents that regex whitespace includes CR;
  this explains the fence regex's tolerance, not all possible line-ending behavior.
- **G1 / G2:** on baseline-PASS records only, change a graded value / delete a key, element or square row.
  A mutation of an ignored extra field or an equal-token swap is not a valid negative control.
- **D1 (PC, not MP):** on baseline-PASS ordinary operation records with turn>1, excluding A-MONO/B/C and
  unchanged previous truth, append the previous state in a fenced block labeled “for reference”. k counts
  capture of that block. This tests last-block **policy conformance**, never semantic invariance.

Nominal exact two-sided CP95 lower bounds compare with 0.95 for the **nine MP/MC families** (Q11/Q12);
a zero-trial family is UNTESTED. All nine pooled rates clear that descriptive bar. D1 is NOT_SCORED,
with its capture interval shown separately. Output totals: 15168 audited verdicts plus 909 policy checks.
**This does not justify iid generalization:** turns share conversations, raw strings repeat, and formats
cluster by model/conversation. There are 1451 unique raw+want+grader inputs, 513 excess duplicates. For
F1/F2, file+pid worst-case composites give **187/194**, nominal CP95 [0.927072,0.985372], INDETERMINATE
against 0.95 — not pooled PASS. Inspect those outputs rather than only pooled FAMILY lines.

**The concrete counterexample:** all 31 multi-fence responses are r8 B cells with operation-8/9/10 states.
F1/F2 leave the operation-9 fence intact after changing the last answer fence; the extractor then grades
operation 9. The 17 originally correct responses flip, so invariance is **14/31** in MULTI and **0/17**
among its baseline-PASS records. Pooled PASS is not absence of this failure class. Removing *all* fences
hides these flips by routing the extractor to a different fallback; that is why the transform span is explicit.

## What this does not establish

- No live model capability, endpoint identity, future-response failure probability or natural frequency of
  formats. R8/R9 historical run-script byte provenance is weaker than R11/R12's recorded machinery pins.
- No proof that last-block extraction is wrong: that policy was intentionally chosen to avoid an earlier
  illustrative fence (`round8-horizon/critic-defects.md:37-39`). D1 violates its answer contract; D1's result
  is policy conformance, not a grader defect or a model improvement.
- No general robustness to bracket-bearing suffixes, deeper nested JSON, invalid JSON or unobserved fence
  variants. Synthetic cases include examples where a bracket-bearing sign-off redirects fallback parsing;
  the F5/F6 bracket-free definition deliberately does not test that class.
- No independent-binomial coverage claim from CP95 over correlated deterministic transforms; per-format
  strata, pid composites and raw+want duplicates expose that limitation rather than repair it statistically.
- No calibration forecast credit: filed probabilities were code-derived expectations, not blind predictions.
- No repair of grader.py: changing its historical extraction policy would alter stored replay semantics.
  A future parser policy needs a separately frozen comparison and explicit treatment of past verdicts.

Falsifier: any pinned candidate whose meta-derived baseline does not replay; disagreement of the independent
oracle on any family count; a transformation called MP that changes the task-defined answer; or a located
source quote that does not support the scope assigned above. These require marking/correcting the entry,
not silently updating a receipt pin or excluding the disagreeing record.

## Decision rule for use

Run the audit when extraction policy changes or a new response-format class enters the corpus. Preserve
baseline truth, exact transform span, conditional denominator and inapplicable reasons. Report pooled and
format-stratified results; do not turn an intentional policy stressor into a correctness defect. Reuse the
existing instrument rather than duplicate the extractor. Reusable testing methodology stays tameshi's;
this lane owns evaluation of its own grader, with evidence in `.ufo/waves/round-26/build/ConnTameshi.md`.
